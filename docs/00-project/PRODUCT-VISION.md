# Product vision

**Status: Accepted** (direction) / **Proposed** (specific milestones beyond the MVP).

## The one-sentence pitch

"Order from your neighbourhood stores. Pick it up yourself, or get it delivered."

## What Dropzo is not trying to be, at launch

- Not a 10-minute delivery promise. Delivery times are based on real merchant preparation time
  and rider availability, not a marketing number.
- Not a dark-store network. No warehouse is rented at launch.
- Not a multi-city platform on day one. One compact service zone first.
- Not a replacement for merchants' own judgement about their stock — merchants manage their own
  prices and inventory.

## Differentiation

Large quick-commerce apps optimise for speed and their own warehouses. Dropzo optimises for:

1. **Local trust** — real neighbourhood shops the customer may already know.
2. **Low merchant friction** — a shop can join without buying new hardware or software.
3. **Pickup as a first-class option** — not an afterthought bolted onto a delivery-first design.
4. **Low operating cost** — merchant-owned stock means Dropzo does not carry inventory risk at
   launch.

## Milestone shape (not a committed schedule)

| Stage          | What "success" looks like                                                      |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Pilot          | 10–20 completed orders/day in Badarpur, with real merchants and real customers |
| Early growth   | Consistent repeat orders, positive contribution margin per order               |
| Expansion      | A second Assam town, using the same codebase and database model, no rewrite    |
| Platform stock | Selective platform-owned inventory for proven high-demand products             |

These are the directional milestones used for prioritisation. They are not committed dates — see
[`06-delivery/ROADMAP.md`](../06-delivery/ROADMAP.md) for what is actually scheduled.
